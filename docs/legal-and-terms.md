# 5050 Games: contest law, disclosures, banned words (Oct 22, 2026)

Not legal advice. Get a lawyer to read the rules before the first payout.

## Verdict
Free entry, no purchase, highest score wins, paid from the site's own ad and merch revenue: a skill contest, not a lottery or sweepstakes, in Canada, the US and the UK. Gambling needs prize + chance + consideration; consideration is removed and chance is made irrelevant by paying the top score. No licence needed. Remaining exposure: (a) an argument that one-tap games are chance-driven (random levels, timing luck), (b) contest-disclosure rules, (c) ad network, app store and PayPal filters reacting to gambling words, (d) proving the winner is an adult before paying.

Quebec note: the Régie stopped regulating contests on Oct 27, 2023. Excluding Quebec is now a convenience choice (French-language rules, Quebec consumer law, Law 25), not a legal necessity. Don't cite the Régie as the reason.

## Five things before the first payout
1. Publish official rules (sponsor identity, eligibility, 18+ and province/state list, exact prize formula, winner selection, tie-break, anti-cheat disqualification, tax responsibility, no purchase necessary) linked from every game screen and social post.
2. Make each week's game deterministic (same seeded level sequence for everyone) and validate scores server-side, so skill decides and chance does not.
3. Collect from the winner before paying: name, address, date of birth, photo of government ID, signed declaration and release, and in Canada a correctly answered skill-testing question.
4. Payment rails: Interac e-Transfer for Canadians from the business bank account; PayPal Business account (ask PayPal to clear the "games of skill and sweepstakes" category) for US and UK.
5. Keep a payout ledger (date, amount, revenue basis, winner, country) for tax and the "proof of past payouts" line on the site.

## Classification by country
- Canada: Criminal Code s.206 covers chance games and mixed chance-and-skill schemes where the entrant pays. No consideration + high-score winner = outside it. Skill-testing question not strictly needed for a true skill contest but costs nothing; add it. Competition Act s.74.06 governs disclosure. https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-46/section-206.html , https://www.smartbiggar.ca/insights/publication/canadian-contests-what-you-need-to-know
- US: most states use the dominant-factor test; all still require consideration for gambling. States that restrict cash-prize skill games (AZ, AR, CT, DE, LA, MD, MT, SC, SD, TN) do so because players pay entry fees. Prize funded by ads, never players, so open nationwide. NY/FL sweepstakes registration applies to chance promos over $5,000, not skill contests. https://www.firstamendment.com/skill-gaming-states/
- UK: Gambling Act 2005 s.14, a competition isn't a lottery if skill prevents a significant proportion from winning, or if entry is free. Both met. https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/guidance/prize-competitions-and-free-draws-the-requirements-of-the-gambling-act-2005

Keep it on the right side: zero consideration (no fees, no purchase, no paid boosts, no "buy merch for extra lives"), skill dominance (deterministic levels, visible scoring, cheats disqualified), non-random tie-break (earliest time the top score was reached), merch never affects ranking.

## Required disclosures
Canada (s.74.06): number and approximate value of prizes, regional allocation, anything materially affecting chances, how winners are selected, prizes without undue delay. Since the prize is a percentage of an unknown number: state the formula, the prior week's payout, and a guaranteed minimum (even $20). Also: sponsor legal name and address, "No purchase necessary", eligibility (age of majority in province, excluded provinces), skill-testing question, closing time in a named timezone, where full rules live.

US: sponsor identity and address, "NO PURCHASE NECESSARY. A PURCHASE WILL NOT INCREASE YOUR CHANCES OF WINNING", eligibility and age, start/end dates, judging criteria (highest verified score, tie-break), prize description and approximate value, "Winners are solely responsible for all taxes", void where prohibited, and the skill-contest odds sentence: "Odds of winning depend on the number of eligible entries and the skill of entrants." Winners list available on request.

## Words to avoid, and what to say instead
Google classifies sweepstakes casinos as gambling and restricts prize-for-play ads; Meta needs written permission for the gambling category; Google Play bans performance-to-money games without approval; Apple requires in-app rules (5.3). Stay a browser site. https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16641934 , https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032

- "cash prize", "prize money" → "the pot", "half the ad money"
- "win real money" → "whoever's on top Sunday night gets it"
- "play to win" → "play, get the high score, get paid"
- "jackpot" → "this week's pot"
- "bet", "wager", "stake" → never; "it's free, there's nothing to put in"
- "guaranteed payout" → "paid every Monday, here's the ledger"
- "earn money playing games" → "the top scorer splits the ad revenue with the site"
- "odds", "chance to win", "lucky" → "skill only, same level for everyone, best score wins"
- "gamble", "casino", "slots", "spin" → never
- "free money", "get rich" → "it's usually a few bucks, sometimes more"
- "sweepstakes", "draw", "raffle" → "weekly high-score contest"
- "entry fee", "buy-in", "tickets" → "free, no account purchase, ever"

## Tax
- Canada: contest prizes are windfalls, not taxable to the winner (CRA Folio S3-F9-C1). No slip, no withholding. Site deducts payouts as a business expense.
- US: ordinary income to the winner. 1099-MISC prize threshold rises to $2,000 for 2026 payments. A Canadian payer with no US trade or business generally needn't file; keep records, and over $2,000 get a W-9. https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102ku4y/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-could-mean-more-valuable-prizes/
- UK: competition winnings not income or capital gains.
- Cross-border: Part XIII withholding doesn't apply to contest prizes.

## Payment rails
- Interac e-Transfer: Canada only; personal limits around $3,000/day, $10,000/week; $0 to $1.50 fee. Use the business account for clean books.
- PayPal: Friends and Family from Canada abroad costs CAD 2.99 plus 4% FX spread. Repeated payouts to strangers can get a personal account limited. AUP lists "games of skill and sweepstakes" as needing pre-approval. Open a Business account, email compliance describing a free-entry skill contest, use Payouts. https://www.paypal.com/ca/legalhub/acceptableuse-full

## Age gating
Age of majority: 18 in PEI, AB, MB, ON, QC, SK; 19 elsewhere in Canada. US 18 (19 AL, NE; 21 MS). UK 18. Minors can legally win but payout and release go through a guardian. An 18+ checkbox is enough to run the contest, not to pay: verify ID before the first payout. Make 18+ a hard account gate (not payout-time) to avoid COPPA (US) and the Children's Code (UK).
